Night Birds

Leave the phone by the window and go to sleep. The page listens all night, BirdNET identifies every bird that calls, and the page answers each one with its own voice and measures how it reacts. When you wake up, a full report is waiting.

Before sleeping: put the phone on its charger by the window with this page open. The screen stays on all night, because phones pause the page when the screen turns off.

Automatic replies

These limits protect the bird. Hearing its own call makes it think a rival is in its territory, and repeating that all night stresses it and drains its energy. The sound may also wake someone at home.

All events

    What will it learn about the bird, and what won’t it?
    • It will learn: which species it is, when and how often it calls, and the shape and pitch of its calls. When the page replies, it learns whether the bird answered, after how many seconds, whether its calling increased afterwards, and whether it got louder (maybe it came closer). That’s exactly what ornithologists measure in playback experiments.
    • It won’t learn: what the bird is saying, thinking, or how it sees the world. No model can translate bird calls into words or thoughts. So the report contains measured behaviour only, never invented sentences put in the bird’s mouth.
    • Replying: As in the Earth Species Project video, timing matters most. The page learns from the bird itself how long it pauses between calls and replies after the same gap. It picks one of the bird’s last 5 calls instead of repeating one recording, because in the video the bird didn’t treat a repeated recording as a real bird. The difference is that the video’s model generated new calls, while this page replays the bird’s own calls. It never replies to human voices, or to sounds below the confidence threshold.
    • Night time: Most birds sleep at night; you’ll hear the most at dawn, when birds start singing. Owls or other night birds will be recorded too.
    • Only sound clips are saved, not the whole night, to save memory. The report is saved on the phone, so if the page closes for any reason you’ll find it under “Last night’s report”.
