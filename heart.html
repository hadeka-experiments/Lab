Heartbeat by Touch

Lie on your back and put the phone on your chest. With every beat your heart shakes your ribs very slightly, and the phone’s motion sensors feel it. Your breathing tilts the phone up and down. No microphone, no camera.

Tap “Start”, then lie down and put the phone on your chest, slightly to the left. The first reading appears after about 10 seconds.
How does it know, and what are the limits?
  • A pumping heart makes the chest vibrate; this is called seismocardiography. The phone measures it with the accelerometer and gyroscope about 60 times per second, and the page looks for a rhythm repeating 45–150 times per minute.
  • Breathing is measured from the phone’s tilt as your chest rises and falls.
  • You must be completely still and silent. Any movement hides the heartbeat, and the page will tell you when that happens.
  • Each number shows a “signal clarity”; if it’s weak, don’t trust the number.
  • This is an experiment, not a medical device.
