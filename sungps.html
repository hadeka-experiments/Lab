Sun GPS

No GPS, no internet, no network: the phone finds where you are on the globe from just the sun’s angle and the clock, the way sailors did 200 years ago.

How to
  1. Stand somewhere open with the sun visible.
  2. Point the back of the phone toward the sun, andlook at the screen, not at the sun.
  3. Hold the phone upright (portrait), and move it until the sun enters the central circle and stays there for about 2 seconds. It records automatically, or tap “Record now”.
  4. One measurement gives a rough location (hundreds of km). Two measurements at least an hour apart are far more accurate, because the sun will have moved.

      How is it calculated, and why isn’t it as precise as GPS?
      • The tilt sensor measures how many degrees the sun is above the horizon, and thecompass roughly which direction it’s in.
      • From the clock the page calculates exactly which point on Earth the sun is directly above at that moment (NOAA equations).
      • Each altitude measurement says you are on a circle around that point. Two measurements at different times = two circles, and you’re where they intersect. The compass picks which intersection.
      • One degree of tilt error ≈ 110 km. The phone’s sensor is accurate to about a degree, so the result is accurate to tens or hundreds of kilometres, not metres. The page calculates and shows the radius of the area you’re most likely in.
      • The page corrects for atmospheric refraction near the horizon, and needs the phone’s clock to be correct (automatic time).
