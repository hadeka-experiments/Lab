Eye Reflections

The cornea is a tiny spherical mirror, and sunglasses are a bigger one; both reflect the scene in front of the person. The page extracts that reflection as a normal picture of the scene: it stacks many frames for sub‑pixel detail, unwraps the cornea’s curve into a flat view, and removes the iris pattern. Optional AI enhancement is shown beside the real picture, never instead of it.

Best result: rear camera at 2× zoom, 20–30 cm from the eye, with the scene the person is looking at well lit. The page stacks 20 frames and aligns them with sub‑pixel accuracy.

Upload several photos of the same person in a row (like Burst shots), or a short, steady video of the face. The page takes up to 30 frames and stacks them, which genuinely improves resolution. A single photo works too, without stacking. Everything runs on the phone; nothing is uploaded.

Tap the reflection in the picture (eye, sunglasses, spoon, car window…) and adjust the box size.

What’s real and what’s a guess?
  • Frame stacking (real): In each frame the reflection is shifted by a fraction of a pixel. The page measures that shift precisely (phase correlation) and aligns frames before stacking, so noise drops and real detail improves a little. This needs several frames or a video.
  • Normal picture (real): The cornea is curved, so each point reflects a different direction. The page computes which direction each output pixel came from and produces a flat view like an ordinary camera. Use the sliders to look left, right, up and down.
  • Remove iris pattern (approximate): The iris is darker than the reflection and its pattern changes slowly. The page estimates and subtracts it, and balances colours so the eye colour doesn’t tint the image. This can eat some large dark objects in the reflection.
  • AI enhancement (a guess): An ESRGAN model upscales 4× and paints details resembling what it learned from other images. Anything that appears in the AI version but not in the real version is invented by the model, such as a face, text or a pattern. That’s why both versions are shown side by side, with the AI one labelled in red.
  • The real limit: how many pixels the iris occupies in the image. The page shows it below. A 40‑pixel iris gives only large shapes; 150 pixels gives much clearer detail.
  • Use it on your own photos, or photos of people who agree. A reflection can reveal where someone is.
