Pulse from Face

With every heartbeat, blood fills the vessels in your face and its colour changes very slightly. Your eyes can’t see it, but the camera can. The page reads that change from your forehead and cheeks and calculates your pulse, without touching anything.

The white screen lights your face, so set brightness to maximum. Hold the phone steady about 30 cm from your face, or rest it on something.
How does it know, and what are the limits?
  • A face‑tracking model locates your forehead and cheeks 30 times per second, and the page averages the skin colour in them.
  • It uses a method called POS, which separates colour changes caused by blood from changes in light and movement.
  • The first number appears after about 10 seconds and updates every second from the last 12 seconds.
  • “Signal clarity” says how visible the pulse is above the noise. If it’s weak, don’t trust the number.
  • Movement, talking and changing light spoil the reading. This is an experiment, not a medical device.
  • Rear camera: measures the pulse of someone in front of you. The screen doesn’t light their face, so the room light must be good, or turn on the flash if your phone allows it. The person should be within 1 m, still, and facing the camera, and ideally know you’re measuring their pulse.
