Pipe Listener

Pressed against a wall or a pipe, the phone hears water moving through your home. Google’s YAMNet model recognises water sounds, and you teach it your own home’s sounds: bathroom tap, toilet flush, washing machine. At night it watches for water running while everyone’s asleep.

Place the phone

Press the back of the phone, or its bottom edge (where the mic is), firmly against a pipe, a wall with pipes in it, or the sink. The firmer the contact, the better it hears.

—

Teach it your home’s sounds

Turn the thing on (tap, flush…) and press record. 8 seconds per example; the more examples at different times, the better it learns. Also record “Quiet” with no water running.

    What happened

    Pipe sound level each minute (the quietest moment in that minute). If the line stays high for hours while everyone sleeps, water is running.

      How does it work?
      • Every second, YAMNet computes probabilities for 521 sound types. The page uses those 521 numbers as a “fingerprint” for that second.
      • When you teach it, it stores the fingerprints for each thing. After that, every second is compared with the stored fingerprints and the closest wins. If none is close enough, it says “unknown”.
      • Leaks: Water flowing through a pipe makes a steady “hiss”. The page records the quietest moment of each minute. If even that quietest moment stays above your “Quiet” for a long time, there is continuous flow. This catches leaks, dripping taps or running toilets, but it can also pick up a nearby motor or air conditioner, so check for yourself.
      • Everything is stored on your phone only.
