Echo Face

The phone emits tiny clicks and listens to their echoes coming back from your face. With no camera and no light, it works out how many layers your face has and how far each is, and can learn to tell faces apart.

1. Room fingerprint without your face

Hold the phone about 30 cm in front of you, screen facing you like a selfie. Then lean your head well away from the front of the phone, without moving your hand, and tap “Record”.

    How does it know, and what are the limits?
    • The click is a chirp sweeping fast from 2 to 20 kHz. The page listens to the echoes and computes how far each one came from.
    • To separate your face from walls and ceiling, the page records the room once without your face and subtracts it from the measurement with your face.
    • Distance resolution is about 2 cm: it tells your nose tip from your cheeks and ears, but nothing finer.
    • The speaker and mic hear in every direction, so the page knows how far away each layer is, but not whether it’s up, down, left or right. That’s why the drawing shows layers, not a picture of a face.
    • Face recognition here is an experiment, not a security lock. Faces of similar size can be confused.
    • The clicks are audible but quiet; each measurement takes about 2 seconds.
