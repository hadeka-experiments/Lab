Bridge Pulse

Every bridge and tall building sways slowly at its own natural “note”, like a guitar string but much slower. The phone’s motion sensor can pick it up, and the page separates it from car and road vibration.

Method
  1. Record a reference: Same conditions but off the bridge: the same car on an ordinary road, or standing on the ground next to the bridge. This teaches the page what “normal” vibration looks like.
  2. Record on the bridge: At least 30 seconds. Best is to be standing on a footbridge, or sitting in a car stuck in traffic on the bridge.
  3. Keep the phone on the floor or the seat, not in your hand.

    How does it work, and what are the limits?
    • The page splits the vibration into frequencies (shakes per second) from 0.3 to 25 Hz. Large bridges usually sway below 10 Hz, and tall buildings below 2 Hz.
    • It compares the bridge recording with the reference. The frequency that is much stronger on the bridge is the candidate for the bridge’s own note.
    • Cars vibrate too: A car’s suspension bounces around 1–2 Hz and the engine faster, so the reference must use the same car. That’s also why a footbridge gives cleaner results.
    • Real confirmation is repetition: If the same frequency shows up in several recordings of the same bridge on different days, it is most likely the bridge’s note. The page collects this below.
    • Browsers read the motion sensor about 60 times per second, so the highest measurable frequency is about 25–30 Hz.
