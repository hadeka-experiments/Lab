    Settings

    Room map

    Stand in the middle of the room and hold the phone in front of your chest with its bottom edge pointing forward. Tap “Start scan” and turn slowly on the spot, pausing every so often until the ring completes 12 directions. Your body blocks part of the echo from behind you, and from that difference the page works out which direction each surface is in.

    ● Hard (wall, wood, glass) · ● Soft (sofa, curtain, bed) · ● Unclear. Object directions are approximate.

    Raw data

    Each dot is an echo, drawn in the direction you were facing when it was measured.

    How to get a good measurement
    • Open the page from an https link; browsers won’t give microphone access to a file opened from local storage.
    • Turn off silent mode, otherwise the chirp won’t play (on iPhone).
    • Keep the room quiet and the phone in your hand away from your body, with its bottom edge (speaker and mic) toward the surface you want to measure.
    • If no echo appears, raise the sensitivity. If many meaningless dots appear, lower it.
    • Phone speakers and mics hear in all directions, so an echo may come from the floor, ceiling or the wall behind you. Your body only partly blocks what’s behind you.
    • “Hard” and “soft” come from comparing the echo at high (8–12 kHz) and low (2–5 kHz) frequencies. Soft things absorb highs more. It only separates hard from soft and doesn’t identify the material. A small hard object may appear “unclear”.
